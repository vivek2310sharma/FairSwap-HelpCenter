Scammers sometimes **claim to be army or other service personnel** when arranging a marketplace deal. A job title, uniform photograph or ID-card image is not independent proof of identity. This advice concerns **impersonation**, not legitimate members of the armed forces.

## Recognise the warning signs

1. Be cautious about someone who relies on an official-looking photo, a claimed posting or urgency to bypass ordinary checks. Images and documents may have been copied or altered.

2. Refuse requests for a refundable "security" deposit, military transport charge, courier release payment or other advance fee before seeing an item. Do not pay merely because someone says a military process requires it.

3. Arrange a safe public inspection where appropriate, verify the item and payment independently, and never disclose an OTP, UPI PIN, bank credentials or identity documents.

4. If the account becomes suspicious, stop the deal. Preserve relevant chat messages and payment references, and report through available FairSwap controls. If money was lost through cyber fraud, contact your bank promptly and use [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) or **1930**.

Do not post a person's claimed ID publicly or assume a genuine profession is itself evidence of fraud.
